# Key Technology Areas

## 1. Earth Observation & Remote Sensing
- Satellites (e.g., Copernicus, Landsat) and drones track deforestation, glacier retreat, air quality, and ocean health in near real time.
- UNEP's **World Environment Situation Room** aggregates satellite and sensor data into public dashboards for policymakers.
- Enables early detection of illegal logging, mining, and fishing.

## 2. Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning
- Predictive models forecast floods, droughts, wildfires, and extreme weather.
- AI-powered image recognition identifies species for biodiversity monitoring and anti-poaching efforts.
- Climate models improve resolution and accuracy of long-term projections.
- AI optimizes energy grids, reducing waste and integrating renewables more efficiently.

## 3. Internet of Things (IoT) & Sensor Networks
- Low-cost air and water quality sensors provide hyperlocal pollution data, especially in underserved regions.
- Smart agriculture sensors reduce water and fertilizer overuse.
- Wildlife tracking collars support conservation and anti-trafficking efforts.

## 4. Renewable Energy & Storage Technology
- Falling costs of solar, wind, and battery storage accelerate the shift away from fossil fuels.
- Smart grids and demand-response systems improve efficiency and resilience.
- Green hydrogen and next-generation storage address intermittency challenges.

## 5. Big Data & Digital Platforms
- Open environmental data platforms (e.g., UNEP's **Global Environment Data Strategy**) improve transparency and accountability.
- Digital MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, Verification) systems track emissions and support carbon markets.
- Citizen science apps crowdsource observations on pollution, biodiversity, and waste.

## 6. Blockchain & Supply Chain Transparency
- Traceability platforms verify sustainable sourcing (timber, seafood, minerals) and combat greenwashing.
- Tokenized carbon credits and payment-for-ecosystem-services schemes improve financing for conservation.

## 7. Biotechnology & Materials Science
- Biodegradable and bio-based materials reduce plastic pollution.
- Enzymatic recycling breaks down plastics that were previously unrecyclable.
- Precision fermentation and lab-grown alternatives reduce land, water, and emissions footprints of food production.

## 8. Circular Economy Digital Tools
- Digital product passports track material composition to improve reuse and recycling.
- Marketplace apps extend product lifecycles (repair, resale, sharing platforms).
- Smart waste management systems optimize collection routes and sorting.
